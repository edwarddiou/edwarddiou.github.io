Research In Development

Domain Knowledge Internalization — Insurance Advisor as a Case Study

A research exploration of an alternative to RAG for structured domains: instead of retrieving knowledge at inference time, jointly train a knowledge graph with a small LLM (Qwen3-0.6B) so that structured product knowledge lives inside the model weights. The assistant answers without any retrieval step at inference.

Insurance is the case study — a domain where retrieval-based answers tend to be "literally relevant but structurally misaligned". The full training pipeline (CLM → SFT → DPO → PPO) runs on a single RTX 3090, with depth on a single product (Taiwan reimbursement-based medical insurance) rather than breadth.

Product In Development

AI Customer Simulator — Multi-step Reasoning for Sales Training

A realistic simulation environment where an AI plays the role of a customer interacting with sales or service staff — complete with dialogue scoring and actionable feedback. The core design uses multi-step reasoning to decompose the customer's response into separate dimensions (persona, mood, motivation, conversation flow) so the simulated customer behaves like a specific person rather than an averaged-out polite assistant.

Major players in the financial industry have expressed strong interest in adopting the system.

2024.09 — 2025.06
Product

Tenfold AI — Legal Advisor Chatbot & Multi-step Architecture

The founding product I built at Tenfold AI: a legal advisor chatbot for a domain where answers must cite the right statute and address the actual question. A general-purpose RAG pipeline isn't enough — the retrieval layer needs to be tuned for legal context, and the answer needs to come with evidence the user can trust.

I designed a multi-step architecture with rerank, regulation check, and other verification tools, capped by a verified confidence score attached to each response — so users not only get an answer, but understand how grounded that answer is in the underlying law.

Master's Thesis

G3-RAG — Multi-step Reasoning RAG over Knowledge Graphs

A graph-guided Retrieval-Augmented Generation framework that addresses information loss in long-context LLMs by constructing a structured knowledge graph via relation extraction and combining it with graph-based retrieval — improving multi-hop reasoning, multi-source integration, and answer accuracy.

Received faculty approval and proceeded to journal submission.
